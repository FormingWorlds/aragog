# Solver method tuning

Aragog dispatches stiff time integration through `_EnergyParameters.solver_method` (see `src/aragog/parser.py`). Three values are accepted: `"cvode"` (default), `"radau"`, and `"bdf"`. This page is the practical recipe for picking one and tuning the associated tolerances.

!!! note "Default is CVODE"
    Production PROTEUS runs rely on `solver_method = "cvode"` with `use_jax_jacobian = true`. The other two values are fallback paths, not equivalents.

## Available integrators

| `solver_method` | Backend | When to use |
|-----------------|---------|-------------|
| `"cvode"` | SUNDIALS CVODE via `scikits_odes` | Default for all production runs. Modified Newton with cached Jacobian; matches SPIDER's solver class. Required for the JAX-traced analytic Jacobian path. |
| `"radau"` | scipy `solve_ivp(method="Radau")` | Pure-Python fallback when `scikits_odes` is not installed. Fully implicit; reliable on smooth profiles but stalls at the rheological transition on long cooling runs. |
| `"bdf"` | scipy `solve_ivp(method="BDF")` | Pure-Python fallback. Cheaper per step than Radau, weaker at sharp Jacobians. Use only for quick smoke tests. |

If `solver_method = "cvode"` is selected and `scikits_odes` is missing, the solver logs a warning and falls back to `Radau`. See [Installation: production solver path](installation.md#production-solver-path-sundials-cvode-jax-analytic-jacobian).

## Picking tolerances

The relevant keys are `solver.atol` (absolute) and `solver.rtol` (relative); `atol` is floored at $10^{-8}$ inside the solver, and `rtol` is used as given. Suggested starting points:

| Regime | `atol` | `rtol` | Notes |
|--------|--------|--------|-------|
| Production coupled run, CVODE + JAX | `1e-8` (from `atol_temperature_equivalent`) | `1e-8` | Matches the PROTEUS defaults for Aragog in `proteus.config._interior`. Tight enough that energy-conservation checks close. |
| Standalone smoke test | `1e-7` | `1e-7` | Lets the integrator march through the rheological transition in seconds. Acceptable for first-run sanity checks; not for paper plots. With CVODE, logs the tolerance warning. |
| Tight verification or parity test | `1e-10` (applied as `1e-8`) | `1e-10` | Useful for SPIDER bit-parity diagnostics. Wall time roughly doubles. |

When a CVODE call stalls or runs far slower than expected, check whether CVODE is locked at the convective switch (the convective mask and the $\kappa_h$ floor). In a lock the step stays at one small value and the entropy difference across one basic node oscillates with period 2 (around zero in the measured locks, all at $D = 0$); in the measured locks CVODE used about 1.4 RHS evaluations per step and evaluated its Jacobian about every 50 to 60 steps (with INFO logging, the `CVODE stats` line shows `rhs_wrap/nst` and a small `last_step`). Two of the runs counted below were run again with every accepted step recorded: both kept a constant step with a period-2 oscillation of the entropy difference at mid-mantle nodes, at 1.41 RHS evaluations per step on the energy_balance core (the difference stayed just below zero) and at 1.14 on the quasi_steady core (around zero); the other counted runs were not checked for the pattern. A lock can run for a long time without an error, or end in `CV_TOO_MUCH_WORK` once `max_steps` is used up in one output interval. Locks were seen at `rtol` `1e-6` and not at `1e-8`; the remedy is `rtol` and `atol` at `1e-8`, and a CVODE run logs one warning when `solver.rtol` is above `1e-8`. The counts below were measured at the default `convective_threshold = 0`, where the switch is at $dS/dr = 0$; the probe did not cover $D > 0$, where CVODE also stalled at `1e-8` (see [Heat transport](../Explanations/heat_transport.md#convection-mixing-length-theory)). They come from a probe variant of the energy_balance isentropic start of `tests/test_phase_boundary_cap.py` (24 nodes, 200 yr, fixed cap unless stated) that draws the initial entropy uniformly between 8000 and 8400 J/kg/K for each run and adds a relative noise of $10^{-15}$; the probe script is not in the repository, and the runs were on macOS arm64. A run counts as locked when it has not reached 200 yr after 600 s of wall time: every run was screened at 60 s, and each run that missed that was run again alone to 600 s, by which time a locked run had used about $1.7 \times 10^5$ RHS evaluations. With `rtol` = `atol` = `1e-6`, CVODE locked in 12 of 1133 runs, in 4 of 474 on the quasi_steady core and in 2 of 286 with the rate cap, where 2 more runs finished only after 233 s and 346 s. With both at `1e-5`, all 80 runs were run to 600 s and none locked (too few runs to show it safe; the slowest finished after 100.6 s and $6.5 \times 10^4$ RHS evaluations), but the median run needed about $1.6 \times 10^4$ RHS evaluations, against about $1.3 \times 10^3$ at `1e-8`. With both at `1e-8`, CVODE locked in none of 1026, 627 and 1224 runs for three setups (fixed and rate cap on the energy_balance core, fixed cap on quasi_steady). With `atol` at `1e-8` and `rtol` at `2e-8`, `5e-8` or `1e-7`, the values of the PROTEUS retry ladder, it locked in none of the runs: 1308 to 1836 for each value and core with the fixed cap, and 620 and 636 with the rate cap at `5e-8` and `1e-7`. These counts hold for the 24-node standalone case, not for a coupled solve. At $D = 0$ the interior flux is continuous at the switch and only its slope jumps, but with `core_bc` `energy_balance` or `gradient` the convective CMB flux jumps by $\rho T \kappa_h^\mathrm{floor}\,|\partial S/\partial r|_\mathrm{cmb}$ when the node above the CMB crosses 0; at $D > 0$ the convective flux stays continuous at 0, but the mixing flux of a mush and the CMB flux jump at 0 and the floor flux jumps at $D$ (see [Heat transport](../Explanations/heat_transport.md#convection-mixing-length-theory)), so the $D = 0$ counts and the `1e-8` remedy do not carry over to $D > 0$. On CI the AVX-512 numpy path made the test's own case, at its single fixed initial entropy, stall at `1e-6`, judged by the CI test timeout rather than by the 600 s criterion. For a stall without this pattern, raise `max_steps`; the PROTEUS retry ladder raises `max_steps` and relaxes `rtol`, and the relaxed `rtol` can itself be above `1e-8` (see [PROTEUS coupling](proteus_coupling.md)). If the stall persists with an effective `rtol` of `1e-8`, the issue is usually a sharp solidus/liquidus crossing, not the integrator class; consider enabling [`phi_step_cap`](phi-step-cap.md). With `convective_threshold` above 0 it can also be a node whose gradient stays near $dS/dr = 0$; the default 0 does not have that jump.

## CVODE specifics

CVODE is selected by default and is the only path that supports the JAX-traced analytic Jacobian (`use_jax_jacobian = true`). With JAX absent, CVODE falls back to a finite-difference Jacobian: correct, but $O(N)$ RHS evaluations per Jacobian build and noisier on stiff profiles. See the [CVODE and JAX explainer](../Explanations/cvode_jax.md).

The tolerance warning also names a configured `atol` above `1e-8`, but only on the `energy_balance` core, the one setup whose lock data show a loose `atol` adding to the lock. No lock data exist for the other core boundary conditions, `core_module` included, so the warning makes no `atol` claim for them.

CVODE also accepts a SUNDIALS root function for melt-fraction step capping; see [`phi_step_cap` how-to](phi-step-cap.md). The scipy fallbacks register the equivalent capping logic as a `solve_ivp` event with `terminal=True`, so the same TOML config behaves consistently.

## Radau and BDF specifics

Both scipy paths use `solve_ivp` with `dense_output=False` and a 1 yr `max_step` near phase boundaries. They do not support the JAX-traced Jacobian; a Jacobian is computed by scipy via finite differences when needed.

With `phase_boundary_cap = "fixed"` the 1 yr `max_step` activates at the same trigger on all three integrators. The default `"rate"` runs only on CVODE: it arms within the larger of `phase_boundary_entropy_margin` and the stiff-zone half-width and integrates the call in segments; the scipy paths fall back to the 1 yr step.

## EOS floor

The entropy state of CVODE has no lower bound, but the EOS tables stop at a minimum entropy $S_\mathrm{min}$; below it every lookup is clamped to the table edge, so a cell keeps losing entropy at a fixed temperature. This happens when a prescribed surface flux, fixed over a coupling step, drains the top cell after it crystallises. The EOS floor (`energy.eos_floor_margin` $m$, default 100 J kg$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$, below the entropy span of the tables) is one more component of the step-cap root function. It watches the smaller of the top basic-node entropy, which sets the surface temperature, and the smallest cell entropy (the CMB basic node is not watched); with `boundary_conditions.top_node = "limited"` it watches the smallest cell entropy only, so the statements below about the rate and the refusal of the top basic node apply to `"linear"`, and it ends the call when that value reaches $S_\mathrm{min} + m$, or $S_\mathrm{min} + m/2$ when the call starts below $S_\mathrm{min} + m$. A start counts as above the level $S_\mathrm{min} + m$ or $S_\mathrm{min} + m/2$ only when it lies more than $10^{-6} m$ above it; the table boundary below uses $2 \times 10^{-6} m$. CVODE then returns status 0 with a shorter `dt_actual`, scipy status 1 at the event, and the stop is logged as a WARNING with its level. A call that starts at or below $S_\mathrm{min} + m/2$ arms a level at half its distance to $S_\mathrm{min}$; it does not advance (status 1, `dt_actual = 0`) when the rate of that entropy at the call start, taken over every component no more than $10^{-6} m$ above that entropy, reaches the level before the call ends. Above $S_\mathrm{min} + 2 \times 10^{-6} m$ the rate of the top basic node is the larger of its own rate and the rate of the top cell: the node is a linear extrapolation of the top two cells and can fall when only the gradient below it steepens. Inside that band its own rate counts. A start at or below $S_\mathrm{min} + 2 \times 10^{-6} m$ also arms half its distance to $S_\mathrm{min}$, but does not advance on any fall of its lowest component, however slow, at every span, nor when no level fits strictly between $S_\mathrm{min}$ and the start. Every start at or below $S_\mathrm{min}$ is refused, also a top basic node extrapolated below $S_\mathrm{min}$ from a steep top gradient while every cell is inside the table and the node or the top cell rises, so no call advances from a top node outside the table. A top node that falls only by extrapolation while the top cell warms therefore advances above the band in calls that each stop at half its distance, at most $\lceil \log_2 (d_0 / 2 \times 10^{-6} m) \rceil$ calls that stop at the floor from a distance $d_0 \le m/2$ (a call too short to reach its level adds one that does not stop), and is refused once inside the band. A call with no span (start equal to end) skips the floor. Between $S_\mathrm{min} + 2 \times 10^{-6} m$ and $S_\mathrm{min} + m/2$ the refusal depends on the call span, so a caller that retries with a shorter span can then advance. A refused or discarded call returns status 1, `dt_actual = 0` and its start state, with a WARNING. PROTEUS treats such a call as a failed attempt: its retry ladder retries with half the span (and up to 5 times the `atol`), which can then advance in that range, so under a drain that does not stop the surface entropy can come closer to $S_\mathrm{min}$ than $m/2$; at the table minimum a shorter span does not advance a falling start. When the ladder runs out, PROTEUS keeps the interior state of the previous step while the other modules advance, and stops the run at the third failure in a row. Above $S_\mathrm{min} + 2 \times 10^{-6} m$, a drain too slow to reach half the distance in the call, a pause or a warming surface continues. The refusal is logged as a WARNING that says the entropy is at or below the table minimum, leaves no level between it and the table minimum, falls at the table minimum, or falls too fast between the band and $S_\mathrm{min} + m/2$, also when the start rate is NaN. A floor stop at the call start, where the level lies within the root location error of the start, is a refusal with its own WARNING.

The floor is armed in every solve with the entropy EOS and $m > 0$, except on the `gradient` core; a call in which it does not fire takes the same steps and gives the same result as without it. CVODE locates a root to a time tolerance of about $100\,u\,(|t| + |h|)$, with $u$ the unit roundoff, $t$ the absolute call time and $h$ the step, so a stop can lie below its level by up to about $|dS/dt| \times 2.2 \times 10^{-14} \times (|t| + |h|)$ ($t$ and $h$ in yr), about $|dS/dt| \times 2.2 \times 10^{-14} \times t$ for steps much shorter than $t$; scipy event location also loses precision with $t$, more slowly; for the 11-cell state of the floor tests under $10^6$ W m$^{-2}$, a stop at $10^9$ yr lay $1.6 \times 10^{-3}$ J kg$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$ below its level on CVODE, $6 \times 10^{-6}$ below it on Radau and $9 \times 10^{-6}$ above it on BDF (a scipy event can stop on either side of its level). A call whose returned states or energy trace hold a top basic-node or cell entropy below $S_\mathrm{min}$ is discarded: it is solved again as a refusal, so its state, time and energy trace stay at the call start, and the WARNING names the time after the call start, and that entropy and the level as offsets from $S_\mathrm{min}$. So every state a call returns is inside the table when the call starts inside it. Trial steps and Newton iterates of the integrator can still evaluate the right-hand side below $S_\mathrm{min}$, with clamped lookups, at any margin; the returned states and the energy trace cannot hold such a state. The floor guards the table range, not its resolution: where a table is flat in temperature above $S_\mathrm{min}$, a stop at the level still gives the clamped edge temperature. In the solid table of the floor tests, $T$ is the same over the first four entropy rows ($S_\mathrm{min}$ to $S_\mathrm{min} + 322$ J kg$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$) at every pressure up to 6 GPa, so with $m = 100$ the floor keeps the state inside the table but the surface temperature at the edge value; in one PALEOS solid table at the lowest pressure, $T$ is the same over the rows from about $S_\mathrm{min} + 70$ to $S_\mathrm{min} + 620$ J kg$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$. PROTEUS has no key for the margin, so coupled runs use the default.

## Top basic node

The top basic node sets the surface temperature `T_magma` and, under the grey-body surface condition, the radiated flux. With `boundary_conditions.top_node = "linear"` (default) it is the linear extrapolation of the top two cells, $S_N + D$ with the half step $D = (r/2)(S_N - S_{N-1})$ and $r$ the ratio of the outer basic spacing to the spacing of the top two cells. Over a cold top cell on a hot cell this extrapolation can leave the entropy table, and the EOS floor then refuses the call. With `"limited"` the half step is reduced by a smooth minimum against half the distance of the top cell to the table edge it moves toward: the node is $S_N + \mathrm{sign}(D)\,M(|D|, b)$, with $b = c\,(S_N - S_\mathrm{min})$ for $D < 0$, $b = c\,(S_\mathrm{max} - S_N)$ for $D > 0$ (0 outside the table), $M(a, b) = a b / (a^p + b^p)^{1/p}$, $c = 1/2$ and $p = 4$. With the top cell in the table the node lies between the top cell and the linear node and keeps at least half the top cell's distance to each edge, so it stays in the table and the EOS floor watches the cells only. Its slope against the top cell lies between $1 - c$ and $1 + r/2$ and its slope against the cell below between $-r/2$ and 0, so a cooling top cell lowers the node and, under the grey-body surface, the radiated flux. Away from the edge the node returns to the linear node: it differs from it by the fraction $1 - (1 + (|D|/b)^p)^{-1/p}$ of $|D|$, below $10^{-3}$ once $b \ge 4|D|$, that is a top cell at least $8|D|$ from the edge. With the top cell in the table the node is smooth across $D = 0$. A top cell outside the table whose half step points away from it is its own node; one whose half step points into the table takes $b$ from the far edge. At the edge the half step points to, the slope changes from 1 outside to $1 - c$ inside. The state, `T_magma`, the grey-body flux and the JAX right-hand side use the same node; the numpy mesh stops the call with an error when a node breaks the bounds above, a check that runs on every state `get_state` reports. The EOS floor margin does not enter the node. The `gradient` core, whose basic entropies come from its own state, refuses `"limited"` when the configuration is built and when the mesh is built; `BoundaryConfig` also refuses an assignment of either value. A node inside the table can still lie in its flat rows: the PALEOS solid table starts at 300 K, and below the entropy of that edge at a given pressure the table repeats the nearest valid value, so a cold top cell at low pressure reports about the edge temperature.
