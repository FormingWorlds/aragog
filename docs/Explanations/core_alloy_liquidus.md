# The core alloy liquidus

The liquidus of the core alloy decides where an inner core freezes: the inner-core boundary lies where the core adiabat meets it. `aragog.core.AlloyLiquidus` gives the liquidus of iron with S, C and O, which lower the melting point, and with Si, H and N, which are treated as lowering it not at all. It has the `t_melt(pressure)` interface of the other melting curves of the core module ([core boundary conditions](core_bc.md)). The `core_module` configuration selects the `iron` or `quadratic` curve; `AlloyLiquidus` is used through the Python API ([tutorial](../Tutorials/core_alloy_liquidus.md)).

## One binary

On the Fe-rich side of an Fe-X binary, pure solid iron is in equilibrium with the liquid alloy. Mori et al. (2017, Eq. 3) and Mashino et al. (2019) describe this liquidus as an ideal solution,

$$
x_\mathrm{Fe} = \exp\left[A \left(1 - \frac{T_\mathrm{Fe}}{T}\right)\right],
$$

with $x_\mathrm{Fe}$ the mole fraction of free Fe in the liquid, $T_\mathrm{Fe}(P)$ the melting curve of pure iron (the PALEOS curve of Anzellini et al. 2013) and $A$ a constant at each pressure. $A$ follows from the eutectic point: at the eutectic composition $w_e$ the liquidus is the eutectic temperature $T_e$, so

$$
A = \frac{\ln x_\mathrm{Fe}(w_e)}{1 - T_\mathrm{Fe}/T_e}.
$$

The liquidus therefore runs from $T_\mathrm{Fe}$ at $w = 0$ to $T_e$ at $w = w_e$. The mole fraction counts Fe against the Fe$_k$X$_m$ units of the alloy, not against single X atoms: FeS for S below 18 GPa and Fe$_3$S above 26 GPa, FeO for O, Fe$_3$C for C below 255 GPa and Fe$_7$C$_3$ above. On the Fe$_3$S units this form reproduces the 3 liquidus curves that Mori et al. (2017, Fig. 9) draw; on S atoms it does not ([validation](../Validation/core_alloy_liquidus.md)). For C the units are not verified: the curves of Mashino et al. (2019, Fig. 8) are closer to straight lines than to this form.

The 2 C units give liquidus temperatures that differ by up to 55 K at 255 GPa. Aragog joins them with a $C^2$ smootherstep over 230 to 280 GPa, so the liquidus has no step; across the blend its slope falls at most 0.76 K/GPa (at 2 wt% C) below the lower of its values at 229 and 281 GPa, just outside the blend, where the slopes are 1.6 to 9.3 K/GPa for 0.5 to 3 wt% C.

The sulfide that coexists with iron at the Fe-S eutectic is FeS up to 14 GPa, Fe$_3$S$_2$ above it (Fei et al. 1997), and Fe$_3$S, which forms between 18 and 21 GPa (Fei et al. 2000). Fe$_3$S units hold at most 16.06 wt% S, less than the eutectic liquid below 17.8 GPa, so Aragog uses FeS units up to 18 GPa and Fe$_3$S units from 26 GPa, joined by a $C^2$ smootherstep of the 2 liquidus temperatures over 18 to 26 GPa; Fe$_3$S$_2$ and Fe$_2$S are not used as units. Both forms run through the same end points, but between them they differ by up to 357.7 K at 21 GPa (9.15 wt% S) and 457.7 K at 18 GPa (10.32 wt% S), where the Fe$_3$S form has zero weight, so the blend adds to the slope of the liquidus, with no data behind it: up to 29.6, 53.6, 81.5, 81.8 and 67.4 K/GPa near 22 GPa for 2, 4, 8, 10 and 12 wt% S, where the slope peaks at 51.4, 73.9, 98.4, 96.7 and 80.0 K/GPa. Over the window the depression per wt% S in the dilute limit falls 3.46 times, from 92.72 K at 18 GPa (FeS units) to 26.80 K at 26 GPa (Fe$_3$S units).

From 1 bar to 15 GPa the measured Fe-S liquidus is not ideal (Buono & Walker 2011; Chen et al. 2008). The ideal form on FeS units misses the measured points: the RMS misfit is 78.60 K at 1 bar (19 points of Friedrich 1910 and Miyazaki 1928, mean $-33.32$ K; the form lies above them up to 11 wt% S and below them from 16 wt% S), 153.15 K at 6 GPa (21 points, mean $-141.36$ K), 88.95 K at 10 GPa (7 points, mean $-82.55$ K) and 150.90 K at 14 GPa (4 points, mean $-12.45$ K). These sets hold the points with S on the Fe-rich side of the eutectic. The 2 pure-iron points at 1 bar, 1800 and 1809 K, lie 22.4 and 13.4 K below the pure-iron curve at 1 bar (1822.4 K); they test that curve, not the form. Between 10 and 14 GPa the measured liquidus falls by 42.05, 39.06 and 15.53 K/GPa at 4, 8 and 12 wt% S (Chen et al. 2008, linear between their points), while the ideal form changes by $+3.05$, $-14.49$ and $-26.89$ K/GPa. The code liquidus falls from 10 to 14 GPa from 4.61 wt% S, the measured one from 1.01 wt% S (linear between the 10 GPa pure-Fe point and the first liquid point at 7.05 wt%), so the code puts the onset of top-down freezing at a higher S content than the data; its pure iron melts 12.9 and 35.0 K above the pure-Fe points of Chen et al. (2008) at 10 and 14 GPa. A non-ideal liquidus below 21 GPa, such as the model of Buono & Walker (2011), is not implemented.

## Several elements

With several elements the mole fraction of free Fe is $x_\mathrm{Fe} = 1 - X$, $X = \sum_i x_i$, and the constant is a harmonic mean of the binary constants weighted by the mole fractions,

$$
\frac{1}{A} = \sum_i \frac{x_i}{X} \frac{1}{A_i}.
$$

For a dilute alloy this gives $T_\mathrm{Fe} - T \approx T_\mathrm{Fe} \sum_i x_i / A_i$, the sum of the dilute depressions of the binaries. Si is treated as lowering no melting point: its atoms count with the Fe of the solvent, so an Fe-Si alloy melts like Fe, and Si dilutes the S, C and O units (6 wt% Si raises the liquidus of a 2 wt% S alloy by 15 K at 135 GPa). Aragog has no liquidus data for H and N, which leave the mole count: an alloy melts, and is held at its eutectic, like the same alloy without its H and N, the rest renormalised.

A composition past the Fe-rich side of the eutectic, $\sum_i w_i / w_{e,i} > 1$, would crystallise a compound, not iron. There the liquidus is held at its value on the boundary, with the composition scaled to $\sum_i w_i / w_{e,i} = 1$, and `AlloyLiquidus.status` flags the pressure (`past_eutectic`).

The curves were read from the vector paths of the published figures and checked by a second read from a raster image; the tables are in `aragog/core/eutectics/`, each with its source in the header. Mashino et al. (2019, p. 140) give about 3 wt% C for the eutectic at 330 GPa in their text; with that value the eutectic composition falls 4 times faster from 270 to 330 GPa than at the end of their curve, and the liquidus of 2 to 3 wt% C falls with pressure there (down to $-4.6$ K/GPa for 3 wt% C). The end of their 330 GPa liquidus curve in Fig. 8, 3.35 wt%, keeps the slope positive and is used. The pressures between the end of each read curve and its anchor at 330 GPa count as data, and the eutectic equals the data up to the end of each range.

The Fe-S nodes are the means of 2 reads of Fei et al. (1997, Fig. 2), which agree to 1.5 C and 0.03 wt%: 1261.25 K and 31.175 wt% S at 1 bar, 1187.9 K and 20.57 wt% at 10 GPa, 1135.25 K and 18.305 wt% at 14 GPa (`fe_s_low_pressure.csv`). Through the nodes and the line ends (1348 K at 21 GPa; the composition table at 20 GPa) $T_e$ and $w_e$ are the monotone cubic Hermite of Fritsch & Carlson (1980): its node slopes are the first chord at 1 bar, the weighted harmonic mean of the 2 secants at 10 GPa, 0 at 14 GPa, where Fe$_3$S$_2$ appears and $T_e$ has its minimum, and the slope of the fit and the table at the join end. The slopes of $T_e$ and $w_e$, and so of the liquidus, have no step at 10 and 14 GPa, which a line between the nodes would put there with no data behind it (up to 38.6 K/GPa for 12 wt% S at 14 GPa). The curve departs from the chord between the nodes: $T_e$ by up to $-25.9$ K at 15.91 GPa, $w_e$ by up to $-0.52$ wt% at 6.67 GPa, so the 6 GPa misfit of the liquidus grows from 142.14 to 153.15 K RMS (22 to 21 points) and the eutectic misses Buono & Walker (2011) at 6 GPa by $-42.2$ K and $-1.44$ wt% instead of $-45.8$ K and $-0.94$ wt%. Over 18 to 21 GPa a smootherstep $b$ of $(P - 18\,\mathrm{GPa})/3\,\mathrm{GPa}$ joins these lines into the Simon fit and the composition table (continued below 20 GPa along its first segment), $T_e = (1 - b)\,T_\mathrm{line} + b\,T_\mathrm{fit}$ and the same for $w_e$, so $T_e$, $w_e$ and their slopes are continuous at 18 and 21 GPa. At 18 GPa $w_e$ is 15.50 wt% S, below the 16.06 wt% that Fe$_3$S units hold (it crosses that value at 17.14 GPa). The Fe-C eutectic below 5 GPa is the cubic through 1416.15 K and 4.3 wt% C at 1 bar with zero curvature there and the value of the Mashino fit and composition table at 5 GPa; $T_e$ takes the slope of the fit there and $w_e$ the slope 0, since the table falls at 5 GPa where the chord from 1 bar rises, so both are monotone (Fritsch & Carlson 1980). $T_e$ rises by 7.09 K/GPa at 1 bar and 43.93 K/GPa at 5 GPa: it is convex, because the join takes the slope of the Mashino fit at 5 GPa, 2.1 times the 21.0 K/GPa that Fei & Brosh (2014, p. 158) measure from 5 to 10 GPa. $w_e$ runs from 4.300 to 4.311 wt%; its slope steps from 0 to the table at 5 GPa, and the liquidus slope of 0.5 to 3 wt% C with it, by $-0.37$ to $-2.60$ K/GPa (2 wt% C: from 36.56 to 34.93 K/GPa).

The data of all 3 binaries start at 1 bar; below it the eutectic temperature would keep its ratio to $T_\mathrm{Fe}$ at 1 bar (homologous) and the eutectic composition its 1 bar value. Above the data the data are taken at an effective pressure $q$ whose slope $dq/dP = 1 - b$ fades from 1 to 0 by a smootherstep $b$ over the 40 GPa above the end of the data (330 to 370 GPa for S and C, 325 to 365 GPa for O): $q = P_\mathrm{end} + 2h\,[z - z^4 (5/2 - 3z + z^2)]$ with $z = (P - P_\mathrm{end})/2h$ and $h = 20$ GPa, so $q$ ends at $P_\mathrm{end} + h$. The eutectic temperature there is $T_e(q)\, T_\mathrm{Fe}(P) / T_\mathrm{Fe}(q)$: it keeps its ratio to the pure-iron curve at $q$ (homologous), not the Simon fit or the Fe-O table at $P$. The composition tables and the Fe-O temperature table continue along their last segment up to $q$. From 40 GPa above the end $q$ stays at $P_\mathrm{end} + h$, and the eutectic composition keeps its value there, 4.81 wt% S, 3.27 wt% C and 15.28 wt% O: the continued tables move the composition by $-0.19$ wt% S, $-0.08$ wt% C and $+0.22$ wt% O from the end of the data. The continued slope is that of the last table segment: for O the last segment of the read curve, for S and C the straight segment from the last read node (275 and 270 GPa) to the 330 GPa anchor, not a measured slope. `AlloyLiquidus.status` flags pressures outside the data (`extrapolated`) for each depressing element in the alloy. The slope of the liquidus therefore changes smoothly into the homologous rule: between points 0.1 GPa apart over 325 to 380 GPa it moves by at most 0.025 K/GPa for the alloys tested. Because the continued compositions move above the data, an alloy whose $\sum_i w_i / w_{e,i}$ passes 1 there crosses its eutectic once, by extrapolation. S and C alloys and their mixtures enter it, since the S and C tables fall: 4.85, 4.9 and 4.95 wt% S at 348, 341 and 335 GPa, the end values 5 wt% S and 3.35 wt% C just above 330 GPa, 3.3 wt% C at 344 GPa, and 4 wt% S with 0.65 wt% C at 333 GPa. O alloys between 15.07 and 15.28 wt% leave it, since the O table rises: 15.2 wt% at 338 GPa, 15.28 wt% at 354 GPa. At the crossing the slope of the liquidus steps up, by 0.3 to 3.7 K/GPa for these alloys. Inside the data, where the alloy is not past its eutectic, the slope keeps the kinks of the table nodes. The largest, between points 0.1 GPa apart, are 3.4 K/GPa for 4.8 wt% S at 275 GPa, where the read curve joins the straight segment to the 330 GPa anchor (1.6 K/GPa for the mixture of 2 wt% S, 0.5 wt% C and 3 wt% O there), 1.0 K/GPa for 3 wt% C at 25 and 270 GPa, and 2.5 K/GPa for 10 wt% O at 55 GPa; they are of the size of the step where an alloy passes its eutectic. At the low-pressure Fe-S nodes the slope has no step; the joins at 18 and 21 GPa (S) add none either, and the Fe-C join at 5 GPa adds the small step of the composition slope above. Alloys past their eutectic at low pressure leave it with a step: the alloy of the tutorial, past its eutectic boundary ($\sum_i w_i / w_{e,i} > 1$, three quarters of the sum from O), at 20.54 GPa (from 27.97 to 41.49 K/GPa) and 10 wt% O at 49.75 GPa (from 10.24 to 15.90 K/GPa).

## Limits

- The ideal form is a fit to the eutectic points, not ideal thermodynamics: for a truly ideal solution $A$ is the entropy of fusion of iron over $R$ and the same for every binary, while over their data the binaries give $A$ from 3.50 (S on FeS, 1 bar; 0.45 at 21 GPa) and, on Fe$_3$S, 2.47 at 18 GPa, where the blend weight of that form starts from 0, and 1.79 at 21 GPa, down to 0.21 at 330 GPa, from 1.73 (Fe$_3$C, 16 GPa) and 0.50 (Fe$_7$C$_3$, 17 GPa) down to 0.40 and 0.12 at 330 GPa, and 1.07 (1 bar) to 3.45 (50 GPa) for O: entropies of fusion of 0.1$R$ to 4.4$R$, against 1.16$R$ for iron in the core module (`ds_fusion` 172.8 J/kg/K). The dilute slopes of the shipped liquidus, $T_\mathrm{Fe} - T$ per wt% as $w \to 0$:

    | Binary (units) | 1 bar | 21 GPa | 135 GPa | 330 GPa |
    |---|---|---|---|---|
    | Fe-S (FeS, blend at 21 GPa, Fe$_3$S) | 9.1 K | 73.5 K | 124.7 K | 510.4 K |
    | Fe-O (FeO) | 59.3 K | 33.0 K | 66.8 K | 137.7 K |
    | Fe-C (Fe$_3$C, Fe$_7$C$_3$ at 330 GPa) | 54.7 K | 65.7 K | 226.9 K | 792.0 K |

- Close to its eutectic the liquidus falls with pressure where the eutectic composition falls fast, since there $dT/dP \approx dT_e/dP - |\partial T/\partial w|\,|dw_e/dP|$: for every S content tested from 4.9 to 7 wt%, between 209 and 275 GPa (down to $-0.64$ K/GPa), along the read curve of Mori et al. (2017, Fig. 5), for 4 wt% S with 1 wt% C at 270 to 273 GPa ($-0.37$ K/GPa), and for 3.28 to 3.37 wt% C between 323.8 and 336 GPa (down to $-0.105$ K/GPa for 3.34 wt%), where the Fe-C table and its continuation fall. A core in this state freezes from the top, which the core module treats as a regime it does not model ([crystallisation regimes](core_bc.md)). Below 14 GPa the Fe-S eutectic temperature falls with pressure (Fei et al. 1997), and so does the liquidus of S alloys from about 4 wt%: 4.8 wt% S at 10.78 to 13.79 GPa (least slope $-2.4$ K/GPa), 8 wt% at 4.99 to 15.57 GPa ($-16.4$ K/GPa) and 12 wt% at 1.74 to 16.41 GPa ($-29.4$ K/GPa); 1 and 3 wt% S keep rising (least slopes 19.5 and 7.2 K/GPa). Up to 4.8 wt% S from 26 GPa, 3.2 wt% C and 10 wt% O from 1 GPa, and for the 2 mixtures tested, the liquidus rises with pressure up to 600 GPa where the alloy is not past its eutectic.
- Only the end points of each binary are data; the liquidus between them follows the form. From 1 bar to 15 GPa the form misses the measured Fe-S liquidus by 79 to 153 K RMS (see above), and the blend of the FeS and Fe$_3$S units over 18 to 26 GPa has no data behind it.
- Below 5 GPa the Fe-C eutectic has 2 data points, 1 bar and 5 GPa; the cubic between them is a choice. Lord et al. (2009, p. 158) give 1420 K at 1 bar, 3.85 K above the value used. Nakajima et al. (2009, p. 207) give the 1 bar point as the eutectic between iron and graphite of Bens & Elliott (1961, cited there), and Lord et al. (2009, p. 158) place it there too, "possibly metastable"; the form keeps Fe$_3$C units there. Ternary data (Hirose et al. 2021) are not used to check the harmonic rule.
- The harmonic rule has no ternary eutectic: a mixture on its eutectic boundary does not melt below the lowest binary eutectic. On a grid of 5151 boundary mixtures of S, C and O the least liquidus is the lowest binary eutectic itself, at the pure binary: 2672 K (S) at 135 GPa and 3713 K (C) at 330 GPa.
- An alloy past its eutectic is held at the boundary by scaling all its S, C and O by one factor, so the held liquidus depends on the whole composition, not on a path of the phase diagram: at 135 GPa, adding up to 12 wt% O to 11.2 wt% S (past its 9.35 wt% eutectic) raises it by up to 238 K.
- The `extrapolated` flag starts at the end of the tables, 330 GPa for S and C and 325 GPa for O, above the highest pressure of each set of experiments: 254 GPa for Fe-S (Mori et al. 2017, p. 135), 255 GPa for Fe-C (Mashino et al. 2019, p. 135) and 204 GPa for Fe-O (Oka et al. 2019, p. 1603). Above those pressures the eutectic comes from the fits, the read curves and the 330 GPa anchors.
- The blend of the 2 C units has no data behind it: at 255 GPa it lowers the slope of the liquidus by 1.5, 2.0 and 1.3 K/GPa (20 to 42 %) below the mean slope of the 2 unit forms, for 1, 2 and 3 wt% C.
- Aragog has no liquidus data for H and N, and they do not enter the liquidus.
- `AlloyLiquidus` is not a melting curve of the core energy budget yet: the budget takes `iron` or `quadratic`, and its outer-core enrichment guard reads the light-element fraction of those curves.
- The solid takes up no light element in this liquidus; solid-liquid partitioning belongs to the core energy budget.
- The slope `AlloyLiquidus.gradient` sets whether a core freezes from the centre: where the liquidus is shallower than the adiabat, iron freezes first at the top ([crystallisation regimes](core_bc.md)). A liquidus that rises with pressure can still be shallower: the adiabat of the default core profile (the core module defaults: central density 12500 kg/m$^3$, length scale 7272 km, thermal expansivity $1.35 \times 10^{-5}$ K$^{-1}$, heat capacity 840 J/kg/K; core radius 3480 km, 136 GPa at the core-mantle boundary) rises by 6.7 K/GPa at 136 GPa and 7.2 K/GPa at 330 GPa for 4137 K at the core-mantle boundary, while the liquidus of 2 and 4.8 wt% S, 2 wt% C and the alloy of the tutorial rises by 2.0 to 5.5 K/GPa at 330 GPa.
