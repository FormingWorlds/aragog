# Reference tables for the core verification page

Both tables are output of the `leeds` core model of [thermal_history](https://github.com/sam-greenwood/thermal_history) (Greenwood et al. 2021; MIT licence) at commit `ea9aa99`, made by `tools/verification/thermal_history_reference.py`. The first line of each holds, as JSON, the inputs, the package versions and the column names. Each row is the state at the start of a 1 Myr step, from a CMB temperature of 4400 K.

- `thermal_history_evolution.csv`: the core alone under a fixed 10 TW CMB heat flow for 1.5 Gyr, with the budget terms thermal_history evaluates on each state. The energy and entropy terms are per unit change of the central temperature, as thermal_history stores them.
- `thermal_history_stable_layer.csv`: the core with the `leeds_thermal` stable layer under the CMB heat flows of the `cases` in its header (fixed flows of 8, 12, -2 and 0 TW for 1 Gyr, and 8, 25 and 8 TW over 400 Myr, which erodes the layer and lets it re-form), with the layer base `r_s` as `depth_r_s` and the depth where the excess over the adiabat falls to 0.1 of its top value as `depth_theta`.

The coupled files come from two PROTEUS runs of `coupled_config.toml`, one with `core_bc = "core_module"` and one with `"energy_balance"`; each row of `coupled_core_module.csv` and `coupled_energy_balance.csv` holds the time (yr), the core temperature and the temperature of the lowest mantle node (K), the CMB heat flux averaged over the call that ends at the row (W m$^{-2}$), the global melt fraction and the relative residual of the core energy ledger; the first line holds the source, the PROTEUS and aragog commits and the column names. The files `coupled_ledger_<case>.csv` hold the core ledger of the `core_module` run at 1 Earth mass (`1me`), of the same run with `interior_energetics.rtol = 1e-10` (`1me_rtol1e-10`) and of runs at 3 and 5 Earth masses (`3me`, `5me`, `planet.mass_tot` changed): per row the time (yr), the core heat change, the CMB heat and the impact heat of the call (J), and the cumulative relative residual; the first line holds the configuration change, the commits and the column names.

The other coupled files come from the same configuration. `coupled_flux_<case>.csv` holds per row the time (yr), the core temperature, the temperature of the lowest mantle node (K) and the CMB heat flux (W m$^{-2}$) of the 3 and 5 Earth-mass runs (`3me`, `5me`) and of a 1 Earth-mass run with the Wolf and Bower (2018) mantle (`wb_1me`). `coupled_core_structure.csv` holds per planet mass the CMB radius (m) and pressure (Pa) of the last row, the core profile of the last snapshot (central density in kg m$^{-3}$, length scale in m, central pressure in Pa) and the final core temperature (K). `coupled_mesh_convergence.csv` holds per run of the mesh test the number of mantle levels, the solver rtol, the basal freezing time $t_\mathrm{bf}$ (yr), the core temperature at $t_\mathrm{bf}$ and at the end (K), and the CMB heat flux at 10, 100 and 1000 yr, at 1, 2 and 4 times $t_\mathrm{bf}$ and at 10, 100 and 500 kyr (W m$^{-2}$); $t_\mathrm{bf}$ is where the melt fraction of the lowest mantle node, linear between snapshots, falls through 0.5, and the temperatures and the call-mean fluxes at a given time are linear in time between the helpfile rows. The first line of each holds the configuration change, the commits and the column names.

`stable_layer_spread.json` holds values of item 13 of the script from runs separate from it: as run, with the start temperature changed in its last bit and with the shell runs at a relative tolerance of 1e-12, on macOS, and the 8 TW centre-temperature difference of a Linux CI run; its header gives the commit and the platform of each run. Item 13 records the changes between these runs for section 13 of the page.

The page numbers that need EOS tables (sections 9 and 12) and the solver tests come from the aragog test tables that CI uses, `spider_eos_test_data.tar.gz` of release `test-data-v1` (sha256 `e3842bc494969571428d7f978d8f70972d40a3bc5d6ac2c09f6a96808f4079fb`), 500 pressures from 0 to 150 GPa by 200 entropies.
